# Physical phenomena and engineering opportunity backlog

This is a parking place for **ideas to look at later**, not a task queue
that runs automatically. It complements
[PHYSICS_FIRST_LEARNING_PLAN.md](../PHYSICS_FIRST_LEARNING_PLAN.md) and
[PHYSICAL_INTERPRETATION.md](../PHYSICAL_INTERPRETATION.md).

The initial entries below are **candidate subjects**, not findings,
designs, validated uses, or commitments to build anything. Their
practical usefulness and relationship to the mathematical construction
remain to be established.

## How to add an opportunity

Record a short, self-contained entry with:

- **ID and category:** PHYS (interesting flow), ENG (potential practical
  use), MEAS (measurement/validation), or VIS (visual explanation).
- **Trigger and relation:** what observation or question brought it up,
  and which learning-plan stage it relates to.
- **Physical question or use:** what might be learned or improved.
- **Smallest useful investigation:** an explanation, calculation,
  diagram, literature review or bounded experiment that could
  distinguish possibilities.
- **Unknowns / caveats:** assumptions, practical constraints and what
  would count as a negative result.
- **Disposition:** "noted for later" (default), "discuss with owner",
  "authorized separately", "completed", or "set aside".
- **Links:** source documents, any approved work and eventual results.

Prefer an answerable question to a vague idea. If an idea overlaps with
a previous entry, add a cross-reference instead of duplicating it.
**Recording is permitted; substantive work requires an explicit user
choice.** A brief conceptual digression within an already authorized
learning discussion is not a new project.

## Physically interesting phenomena

### PHYS-01 — Vortex stretching versus viscous diffusion
- **Trigger / related stage:** Stage 2; what can actually narrow a core?
- **Question:** How does imposed strain compete with diffusion in a
  classical stretched vortex? How do core radius and vorticity respond
  when strain changes?
- **Smallest investigation:** a diagram and dimensional balance for a
  specified conventional vortex model.
- **Caveat:** a prescribed strain field is not a demonstrated
  finite-vessel flow or the paper's blowup mechanism.
- **Disposition:** Noted for later; overlaps the central Stage 2 lesson.

### PHYS-02 — Vortex breakdown and recirculation
- **Trigger / related stage:** Stages 2–3; limits of strong swirl.
- **Question:** When can swirling flow develop breakdown or a
  recirculating region rather than further core concentration?
- **Smallest investigation:** compare a few documented flow regimes
  and identify the governing geometry, swirl and Reynolds scales.
- **Caveat:** actual thresholds depend strongly on setup; no
  universal breakdown threshold is asserted here.
- **Disposition:** Noted for later.

### PHYS-03 — Where does circulation and vorticity come from?
- **Trigger / related stage:** Stage 1; distinguish forcing,
  vorticity production and subsequent transport.
- **Question:** How do boundaries, viscosity, stretching and fluid
  motion create or redistribute vorticity in a real apparatus?
- **Smallest investigation:** a control-volume explanation with an
  example of boundary-generated swirl.
- **Caveat:** do not conflate vortex strength, core size and net
  circulation.
- **Disposition:** Noted for later.

## Potential engineering usefulness

### ENG-01 — Mixing and transport by swirling flow
- **Trigger / related stage:** Stages 2–3.
- **Potential use:** mixing or transport in a vessel, where flow
  topology matters more than extreme core concentration.
- **Smallest investigation:** specify a simple mixing objective,
  identify measurable transport/mixing metrics and compare proposed
  flow patterns.
- **Caveat:** increased swirl does not automatically improve mixing
  or energy efficiency; a useful outcome needs evidence.
- **Disposition:** Noted for later.

### ENG-02 — Vortices, pressure and cavitation around fluid equipment
- **Trigger / related stage:** Stage 3, when estimating pressure
  scales and discussing core limits.
- **Potential use:** understanding vortex-related flow problems
  near pump intakes or other hydraulic components.
- **Smallest investigation:** a grounded example linking observed
  swirl to pressure, free surfaces or cavitation risk.
- **Caveat:** no specific equipment performance or cavitation
  threshold is inferred from the current mathematical model.
- **Disposition:** Noted for later.

### ENG-03 — Boundary-driven swirl and control authority
- **Trigger / related stage:** Stages 3–4; difference between body
  forcing and boundary actuation.
- **Potential use:** estimating which motions or ports can influence
  measurable interior swirl or strain.
- **Smallest investigation:** a simple idealized geometry with
  actuator input, measurable response and an explicit energy budget.
- **Caveat:** current repositories do not validate that a finite
  actuator system reproduces the proposed concentrating mechanism.
- **Disposition:** Noted for later.

## Measurement and verification

### MEAS-01 — Measuring a vortex core without assuming its size
- **Trigger / related stage:** Stages 1 and 3.
- **Question:** How should core radius be inferred from measured
  velocity/vorticity profiles, and how large is the uncertainty?
- **Smallest investigation:** compare two or three operational
  definitions using transparent sample profiles and discuss optical
  measurement limitations.
- **Caveat:** tracer images alone do not establish the full
  three-dimensional flow or pressure field.
- **Disposition:** Noted for later.

## Visualization and education

### VIS-01 — Show velocity, vorticity and tracer motion separately
- **Trigger / related stage:** Stages 1–2; the old image looks like
  an uninformative tank of water.
- **Question:** What simple figure would show the role of radial
  inflow, axial stretching, swirl and viscous spreading without
  confusing prescribed motion with a solved fluid flow?
- **Smallest investigation:** an annotated static schematic with
  the equations and assumptions alongside it.
- **Caveat:** diagrams should identify model assumptions and should
  not imply that the Navier–Stokes research has validated the motion.
- **Disposition:** Noted for later.

## Review procedure

At each stage review, bring forward only the most promising **new**
opportunities. An entry can remain "noted for later" indefinitely.
If no interesting opportunity arises, say so; there is no quota.
Selection for active work, including a side experiment or a separate
repository, belongs to the owner and should be requested explicitly.
